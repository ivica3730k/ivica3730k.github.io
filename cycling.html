01 / Volume

Each year, roughly triple the kilometres of the last.

The headline isn't ride count — it's ride length. Across all three years the number of rides per year stayed remarkably stable at around 66. What changed was the size of each ride.

In 2023 the average ride was 13 km. By 2024 that had climbed to 26 km. In 2025 it was 44 km. The riding shifted from short spins into proper endurance work — and the elevation numbers shifted with it, from under 5,000 m of climbing in 2023 to nearly 30,000 m in 2025.

02 / Effort

Speed and heart rate, month by month — and why the raw numbers lie.

Average speed dipped over time. Average heart rate dropped too. Both of those things, in isolation, mean nothing — they're confounded by terrain. As the rides got hillier, raw speed naturally fell.

The interesting window is May to August 2025: average HR fell to 137–141 bpm while monthly volume was at all-time highs and the rides were genuinely mountainous. That's the aerobic-base signature — more work, less cardiac strain.

03 / The long rides

On 50 km+ efforts, terrain-adjusted, the curve rises then falls.

To compare long rides fairly, each ride's speed and HR were measured against a regression on terrain difficulty (metres climbed per kilometre). The chart below shows the residuals — how much faster or slower than expected for the terrain.

Above zero on the green line means faster than the terrain predicts. Below zero on the pink line means lower HR than the terrain predicts. Both at once means fitter.

04 / The dip

Late 2025 onwards — fewer rides, slower paces, higher heart rates.

From November 2025 the residuals turn negative on both axes. The most telling ride is 21 February 2026: speed −1.6 km/h, HR +12 bpm, on a relatively gentle 56 km route. Slower at higher heart rate is the textbook detraining signature.

The good news is in the last data point. 26 April 2026: speed +1.2 km/h, HR −6 bpm. One ride doesn't make a trend, but it's the right shape.

The verdict

Three years of consistent volume growth, with a clear peak between March and September 2025 when the rides got long, hilly, and fast all at once.

The slowdown that followed shows in every metric — frequency, distance, and effort-adjusted pace. Whatever caused it, the data says it was real, not just a bad month.

And the most recent ride looks like the start of a return.
