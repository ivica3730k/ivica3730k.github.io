02 / A note on heart rate

The 2024 sensor was broken.

HR data from 2024 is unusable. The sensor reads a flat 132 bpm for the entire 7-hour ride — average equals max equals minimum. Either the chest strap died at the start or it was reading cadence. So this analysis compares speed and climbing performance across both years, but heart rate is only available for 2025 (avg 159, max 195 — a genuinely hard day).
03 / The climbs

Slower on the punchy ones. Faster where it counts.

The route has nine matchable climbs. In 2024 you hammered the short ones — VAMs of 514, 516, 497 on climbs under two kilometres are higher than anything you've matched on a repeated climb since.

In 2025 you backed off the punchy efforts and rode the long sustained climbs harder. Climb 5 — the longest of the day at 2.9 km, 4.7% — was 2.2 minutes faster. Same on climbs 6 and 9.

04 / The verdict on speed

The slowdown wasn't on the hills. It was between them.

Total time spent climbing across the matched climbs: 118.8 min in 2024 vs 118.9 min in 2025. Identical. Average VAM across all detected climbs: 422 vs 420. Also identical.

So the 0.7 km/h drop in average speed isn't coming from the climbs. The total ride was 8 minutes shorter in 2025 (less time stopped — 50 min vs 63 min stationary), but moving time went up by 8 minutes for slightly less distance. The flat sections were slower in 2025. Possible explanations: wind, bunch tactics, fitness focus on climbs over flats, or just having a less aggressive pacing day.

The verdict

2024 was a punchy, aggressive ride with a broken HR strap and ridiculous VAMs on the short climbs. 2025 was a more even effort with real fitness data backing it up — and on the climbs that actually matter (the long sustained ones), you were faster.

If you compare on time-spent-climbing alone, the two rides are dead level. If you compare on the long climbs — the closest thing to a fitness test — 2025 was clearly the better ride.
